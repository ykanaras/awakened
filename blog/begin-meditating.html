How to Begin Meditating Without Trying to Empty Your Mind

One of the most common misunderstandings about meditation is that you need to stop thinking. You don't. The practice begins by noticing.

Sit somewhere reasonably comfortable. You don't need a special cushion, a perfectly quiet room, or a particular posture. Let your body be upright enough to remain awake and relaxed enough that you are not fighting it.

Choose one place to return

For a few minutes, let your attention rest on something simple. The natural breath is a useful anchor. You might notice the movement of your abdomen, the feeling of air at the nostrils, or the sensation of your body making contact with the chair.

Sooner or later, your attention will move somewhere else. You may begin planning, remembering, worrying, or wondering whether you're doing meditation correctly. This is not a failed meditation. This is the moment when practice becomes visible.

Notice. Then return.

When you realize the mind has wandered, acknowledge it without scolding yourself. Then gently return attention to your chosen anchor. You may do this dozens of times. Each return is part of the practice.

TRY THIS TODAY

Set a timer for five minutes. Feel three natural breaths. When attention wanders, notice where it went and come back to the next breath. Nothing more is required.

Let five minutes be enough

A sustainable practice does not need to begin with long sessions. A few attentive minutes practiced regularly can help you become familiar with the act of pausing and noticing. You can allow the practice to deepen naturally rather than turning it into another task to perfect.

Meditation is less about creating a special state and more about learning to meet experience with awareness. Start where you are. Notice what is here. Return when you wander.
